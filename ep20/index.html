Anchor Verse

“The queen of the South will rise up at the judgment with this generation and condemn it, for she came from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon, and behold, something greater than Solomon is here.”

Matthew 12:42 ESV

Jesus says this to a crowd demanding a sign. They have the Son of God standing in front of them, and they’re still shopping for better options. The queen of Sheba crossed a continent for Solomon’s wisdom. She recognized it and responded to it. The question this episode puts on the table is whether we do the same thing as that crowd — and whether we even notice.

Word Study σοφία Sophia — SOH · fee · ah —
wisdom • skill • understanding

The same word used for Solomon’s legendary wisdom — and for Christ himself, whom Paul calls “the wisdom of God” (1 Corinthians 1:24). When Jesus points to himself as greater than Solomon, he’s not offering a competing source of sophia. He is the source. All the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden in him (Colossians 2:3).

01
The Question

Where We Actually
Go First

Most of us have a default when we need wisdom. A trusted friend. A podcast. Our gut. Google. These aren’t all bad. But Jesus makes a startling claim, and the crowd he makes it to proves the point: you can have the answer standing right in front of you and still walk away looking for something else.

Two ways to face the same moment
The Crowd

“Show us a sign.”

They had something greater than Solomon in front of them and demanded more proof. Looking to him felt insufficient. They wanted what they could evaluate on their own terms.

Matthew 12:38 · Proverbs 14:12

The Queen of the South

“She came from the ends of the earth.”

She recognized wisdom when it was in front of her and crossed a continent to sit under it. No convenient access, no guarantee of what she’d find. She went anyway.

1 Kings 10:1–9 · Matthew 12:42

Where we go first reveals who we functionally trust. The habits are worth paying attention to. When a decision is hard and the pressure is on, what you reach for tells you more about your actual beliefs than what you say you believe.

The crowd had proximity but not posture. Access to Jesus was not their problem. How they approached him was. Posture matters as much as proximity.

James offers a direct invitation. If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God. He gives generously, without reproach. The barrier isn’t on God’s end. The barrier is whether we actually ask.

Matthew 12:38–42 James 1:5 Proverbs 2:1–6 Matthew 6:33
02
The Narrative

Wisdom Woven
Through Scripture

Wisdom runs through the entire biblical story, from the garden to the cross, and every thread eventually points to the same place.

Wisdom Through the Biblical Narrative
Gen The Break “You will be like God, knowing good and evil.”

Genesis 3:5. God defined what was good. The serpent offered a different deal: decide for yourselves. They took it. This is where the problem of going somewhere other than God for wisdom begins.

OT The Foundation “The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom.”

Proverbs 9:10. The fear of the Lord is a posture, not a knowledge set. Rightly ordered affections produce rightly ordered lives.

Sol The Warning “Solomon’s wisdom surpassed the wisdom of all the east.”

1 Kings 4:30. Solomon had unmatched wisdom and still walked away from God. Knowledge without allegiance doesn’t hold.

NT The Fulfillment “Christ Jesus, who became to us wisdom from God.”

1 Cor 1:30. Everything Proverbs and the wisdom literature were pointing toward arrives in a person. The whole thread finds its end in him.

The fear of the Lord is orientation. It’s the posture of someone who has decided that God is the beginning and end of what is true. Everything else gets evaluated from there.

Solomon’s wisdom was extraordinary and he still walked away from God. That’s what his story carries. Wisdom without allegiance eventually collapses. The wisest man in history ended in idolatry because he stopped going to God first.

In Christ are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge. All of them. Colossians 2:3 is a declaration. Every pursuit of wisdom that bypasses Jesus ends short of where the thread leads.

03
The Honest Part

What Makes It Hard
to Go to Jesus First

We know in theory that Jesus is the source of all wisdom. We affirm it. We’ve sung about it. But in the moment of a real decision — a hard conversation, a fork in the road, something that actually costs something — most of us don’t go to him first. The question is honest enough to sit with: why not?

We trust what we can measure. A trusted friend gives you feedback you can weigh. A podcast episode gives you frameworks. Prayer gives you... silence, often. Or something quiet and hard to hold. We gravitate toward sources that feel more concrete.

We go to people who might agree with us. Seeking wisdom from the Lord requires willingness to receive an answer you didn’t expect — or one you didn’t want. That’s harder than asking someone who will validate what you already think.

The discipline of seeking requires slowness. Our lives reward speed. Wisdom from God often requires sitting, waiting, returning, listening. The cost is time we feel we don’t have.

But James says to ask without doubting. He gives generously to all without reproach. The barrier isn’t on God’s end. And the Spirit of God is given to us precisely so that we can know the things freely given us by God (1 Corinthians 2:12). The access is real. The question is whether we use it.

Journal prompts

Take these one at a time. Don’t rush the first one to get to the second. These are worth sitting with — grab a journal and write out your honest answers.

01

When you need wisdom, where do you tend to go first? Be specific — not “God,” but what actually happens in the first five minutes of a hard decision.

02

The crowd in Matthew 12 had something greater than Solomon in front of them and still missed it. Where in your life right now might you be doing the same thing?

03

Solomon had more wisdom than anyone alive — and it didn’t keep him from walking away from God. What does that tell you about what you actually need?

04

James says to ask God for wisdom and he gives generously without reproach. Is there something you haven’t brought to him yet? What’s actually kept you from asking?

Your next step

This week, go to Jesus first.

Not as a formula. As a practice. Pick one real thing you’re carrying — a decision, a conversation, a direction you’re uncertain about — and take these steps in order before you do anything else with it.

01

Name it. What is the one thing you actually need wisdom about right now? Write it down specifically — the more concrete, the better.

02

Go to Jesus first. Before you text someone, search it, or make a move — bring it to him. Pray James 1:5 over it directly: “Lord, I lack wisdom for this. Give it to me.”

03

Sit with Scripture. Look up one or two passages that speak to the situation. Not for information to apply — for the presence of the one who is wisdom himself.

04

Notice what you were tempted to do instead. That moment of awareness is worth paying attention to. It shows you where your functional trust actually lives — and that’s the starting place for change.

Key passages
Matthew 12:42

The anchor. Jesus says something greater than Solomon is here — and the crowd misses it. The queen of Sheba crossed a continent for less. The question is whether we’re willing to cross the room.

James 1:5–8

If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God. He gives generously without reproach. Ask without doubting. This is the most direct invitation in Scripture to seek wisdom from God himself.

1 Corinthians 1:24–30

Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God. He became wisdom to us. Every search for wisdom that ends somewhere other than Jesus ends short of the substance.

Colossians 2:3

In Christ are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge. All of them. Whatever you’re looking for, it’s in him.

Proverbs 9:10

The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom. Wisdom starts with posture, rightly ordering your life with God at the center. Everything else flows from there.

Proverbs 2:1–6

Seek wisdom like silver. Call out for it, search for it. The posture of the queen of Sheba applied to the ordinary rhythms of daily life.

John 16:13

The Spirit of truth will guide you into all truth. The Spirit who lives in you, ready to lead into God’s wisdom in everyday life.

1 Kings 10:1–9

The queen of Sheba tested Solomon, heard his wisdom, and was overwhelmed. She came looking and she found. Jesus holds this story up as an example of what it looks like to actually seek wisdom.
