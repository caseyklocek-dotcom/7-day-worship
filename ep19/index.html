Surrender

What would it look like to hand everything over — not someday, but this week?

Anchor Text Luke 22:42
Episode June 1, 2026
Begin Study
Anchor Verse

"Father, if you are willing, remove this cup from me. Nevertheless, not my will, but yours, be done."

Luke 22:42 ESV

This is the prayer underneath every ordinary morning. When you wake up and offer God your thoughts, your actions, your plans and agenda, you are praying with Jesus in the garden. A posture you return to every day, in every circumstance, wherever the day takes you. Not my will, but yours.

Word Study θέλημα Thelema — THEL · ay · ma —
will • desire • purpose

The word Jesus uses in "not my thelema, but yours." Your deepest will. Your plans, your preferences, what you most want for yourself. Surrender asks you to place all of that in the Father's hands.

"Not my will,
but yours, be done."

The simplest prayer of surrender is also the bravest. Jesus showed us what it costs and what it produces.

01
The Practice

What Surrender
Actually Looks Like

The Christian life takes the shape of surrender, a daily return to a posture you've already chosen. Picking up the cross means coming back every morning.

The Exchange · Galatians 2:20
1 Die to Self "I have been crucified with Christ."

The old self — who holds the reins and decides what's good — is put to death. This is the starting place.

2 Submit to God "I no longer live, but Christ lives in me."

The space the old self occupied gets filled. Surrender is exchange. What you hand over, the Father returns with something more.

3 Joy, Peace & Hope "May the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace."

The fruit that grows when you stop carrying what was never yours to carry. Romans 15:13

The Christian life requires surrender. It is the shape of the life Jesus described when he said: pick up your cross and follow me. This is the daily practice of those who follow him.

Surrender makes room for something better. What the old self held onto gets replaced. The space fills. That shift changes how the whole thing feels.

Joy, peace, and hope are the fruit of it. Romans 15:13 describes what happens when you stop carrying what was never yours to carry. The God of hope fills the space.

02
The Moment

Hold On
or Let Go

Every act of surrender starts with a choice. In that moment — before the prayer, before the release — there are two paths. You've seen them before. They've been there from the beginning.

Which leaves us with two choices
The Tree of Knowledge

"If I stop deciding, it falls apart."

Taking the fruit of your own understanding. Deciding that you know better — what's safe, what's wise, what you can afford to lose. It looks like wisdom. It produces isolation.

Genesis 3:6 · Proverbs 14:12

The Tree of Life

"God leads where I cannot see."

Trusting the One whose wisdom is greater. The fear of the Lord is the posture of someone who has decided to trust God's wisdom over their own.

Proverbs 3:5–7 · Proverbs 9:10

Surrender is a choice, and you don't make it alone. In the moment when holding on feels easier, the Holy Spirit is ready to give you what you need to let go. Asking for that help is the most honest thing you can do.

Submitting your will to His covers everything. The conversation you're dreading. The plan you're holding too tightly. Whatever it encompasses — it's on the table.

Proverbs 3:5–7 Proverbs 9:10 James 4:7 Romans 8:26
03
When It's Hard

For the Days Surrender
Feels Impossible

There will be days when surrender feels like tearing. Those are the moments where the gospel becomes most real, where what you're trusting gets tested and proves true.

You are not alone in this. The Holy Spirit is your active Helper, ready to give you what you need to do the will of God. He intercedes when you don't have the words.

Surrender leads to life. Jesus said it plainly: whoever loses their life for his sake will find it. What feels like loss is the doorway into something more.

God's asks are always for your good. When he invites you to let go, it is a door. He works all things together for the good of those who love him. Every ask is evidence of that.

Jesus is our perfect example. He asked if the cup could pass from him — and still he surrendered. He knows what it costs, and he understands the weight of these moments in ways no one else can. You don't have to pretend this is easy.

Journal prompts

Take these one at a time. Don't rush the first one to get to the second. These are worth sitting with — grab a journal and write out your honest answers.

01

What is one area of your life right now where you're holding on? What are you afraid would happen if you let go?

02

What would it look like to hand that area over to Jesus this week — not someday, but specifically this week? What would the first step actually be?

03

Think about the two-trees moment — when you're tempted to decide for yourself what is good rather than trusting God's lead. Where does that show up most in your life right now?

04

The Holy Spirit is your Helper when surrender feels impossible. What would it look like to ask him for that help today — in a specific situation, not in general?

Your next step

This week, practice handing it over.

Not perfectly. Not all at once. Pick one thing and stay with it. These four steps move in order — start where you are and work forward from there.

01

Name it. Identify the one area you've been holding on to. Write it down — get it out of your head and into the light.

02

Take a first step. Don't wait for the perfect moment. What's one concrete thing you can do this week to begin handing it over?

03

Watch for the two-trees moment. Notice when you're tempted to decide for yourself what's good. That moment of awareness is the beginning of surrender.

04

Ask the Holy Spirit for help. In a specific situation, not in general. Start each morning this week with that one ask.

Key passages
Luke 22:42

The anchor. Jesus in Gethsemane — the honest ask, the naming of the cost, the hinge word, the surrender. The prayer underneath every ordinary morning.

Romans 12:1

Presenting your body as a living sacrifice is your spiritual worship. Surrender is the shape of the everyday.

Galatians 2:20

The three-part progression: crucified with Christ, Christ living in you, life now lived by faith. Each phrase is a step into something more.

Romans 15:13

The fruit of surrender: joy, peace, and hope — not from holding tighter, but from letting go into the hands of the God of hope.

Proverbs 3:5–7

Trust in the Lord with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding. The two-trees choice in one verse.

Romans 8:26

The Spirit helps us in our weakness, interceding when we don't know how to pray. You are not alone in the struggle to let go.

Romans 8:28

All things work together for good for those who love God. Every invitation to surrender is grounded in this promise.

Hebrews 4:15

We do not have a high priest who cannot sympathize with our weakness. Jesus has been in this moment. He knows.
