7 Day Worship · Episode 22 Study Guide

Abide

The fruit you keep straining to produce was never yours to force. It grows on a branch that stays connected.

Anchor Text John 15:5
Episode June 22, 2026
Begin Study
Anchor Verse

"I am the vine, you are the branches; he who abides in Me and I in him, he bears much fruit, for apart from Me you can do nothing."

John 15:5 NASB

Jesus said this in the upper room, the night before the cross, with only hours left to teach his closest friends. He did not hand them a strategy. He handed them a picture. A branch does one thing, and it stays joined to the vine. Everything else, the growth and the fruit and the life, flows out of that single connection.

Word Study μένω Menō — MEN · oh —
abide • remain • stay

Jesus repeats this one word eleven times in John 15. To menō is to make your home in a place and stay there. Abiding asks less about trying harder and more about not leaving.

"Apart from me
you can do nothing."

The hardest sentence to believe when you are capable and driven. Also the most freeing, once you finally do.

01
The Practice

What Abiding
Actually Looks Like

A branch does not strain to produce grapes. It stays joined to the vine, and the fruit comes. Abiding takes the same shape, a daily return to the one connection that everything depends on.

The Connection · John 15:5
1 The Vine "I am the vine."

Jesus is the source. Every bit of life that moves through you starts in him. Nothing about your fruit originates with you.

2 The Branch "You are the branches."

Your one job is to stay joined. The branch does not manufacture life. It receives it, and it lets the life flow through.

3 The Fruit "He bears much fruit."

What grows is the evidence of connection. Fruit is the overflow of a life that stayed close to the vine. John 15:5

The Christian life grows out of connection. Jesus did not say work harder for me. He said stay joined to me. Fruit is the byproduct of a branch that never leaves the vine.

Abiding is daily and ordinary. It looks like a Tuesday morning prayer, a verse carried into a hard meeting, a quiet return to him in the middle of a normal week. Small, repeated, and easy to overlook.

The fruit is not yours to force. When you stay close, things grow that you could never have produced on your own. Love, patience, a steadiness that surprises the people around you. That is the vine at work in the branch.

02
The Moment

Striving
or Abiding

Every ordinary day hands you the same quiet choice. You can carry the weight yourself, or you can stay close and let him carry it. The difference rarely looks dramatic. It shows up in where you turn first.

Which leaves us with two branches
The Branch That Strains

"If I don't make it happen, it won't."

Trying to produce the life of God by your own effort. It feels responsible. It looks like faithfulness. And it slowly wears you thin, because a branch was never built to be its own source.

John 15:5 · Isaiah 40:30

The Branch That Stays

"I stay close, and he provides."

Trusting that the life you need flows from him. The fear of running dry loosens its grip once you remember you were never the source in the first place.

John 15:4 · Jeremiah 17:7–8

Abiding is a choice you make again and again. The branch does not decide once and coast. It stays through dry seasons and full ones, through the days you feel close and the days you feel nothing at all.

Drifting happens quietly. No one decides to walk away from the vine. You get busy, you get tired, the connection thins out a little at a time. Naming the drift is the first step back.

John 15:4 Jeremiah 17:7–8 Hebrews 3:13 Luke 10:42
03
When It's Hard

When Abiding Feels
Like Losing Something

Jesus said the Father prunes every branch that bears fruit so it will bear more. Pruning feels like loss while it is happening. It is the vine's care, aimed at a harvest you cannot see yet.

Pruning is not punishment. The Father cuts back what is good so that what is better can grow. When a season strips something away, it may be his hand making room for more fruit, and not less.

You are not the one keeping yourself connected. The vine holds the branch. On the days your faith feels thin, the connection does not depend on the strength of your grip. He holds you.

Dryness is not the end of abiding. Even branches in winter stay joined to the vine. Feeling far from God is not proof you have left him. Stay put, keep returning, and the sap rises again in time.

Jesus abided first. He said he only did what he saw the Father doing. He stayed so close to the Father that their wills were one. The life he is inviting you into is the life he already lived.

Journal prompts

Take these one at a time. Don't rush the first one to get to the second. These are worth sitting with, so grab a journal and write out your honest answers.

01

Think of a season when you were genuinely close to Jesus. What did that actually look like day to day? What was different about you in it?

02

Where do you notice the gap between going through the motions and actually staying connected? What usually gives it away in your own life?

03

When have you realized you had drifted from him? What brought you back, and what would help you notice sooner next time?

04

Is there fruit in your life right now that only exists because you have been staying close to Jesus? Name it, and thank him for it.

Your next step

This week, practice staying joined.

Abiding is not a project to complete. It is a connection to keep. Pick one of these and let it be the small, repeated way you stay close this week.

01

Pick a fixed return point. Choose one moment in your day, the first coffee, the commute, the minute before bed, and make it the place you turn back to him. Same time, every day.

02

Carry one verse. Take John 15:5 into your week. Let it surface in the meeting, the conversation, the moment you start to strain. Let it pull you back to the vine.

03

Name your drift. Where have you been running on your own strength? Say it plainly to God. Naming it is how the branch finds its way back.

04

Watch for the fruit. At the end of the week, look back. What grew that you did not force? That is the vine at work, and it will build your trust for the week ahead.

Key passages
John 15:5

The anchor. The vine, the branches, the fruit, and the sobering line that holds it all together: apart from me you can do nothing.

John 15:1–2

The Father is the gardener who prunes every fruitful branch so it bears more. Abiding includes seasons of being cut back.

John 15:4

Abide in me and I in you. The branch cannot bear fruit by itself. The whole invitation in a single verse.

John 15:9

As the Father has loved me, so I have loved you. Now remain in my love. Abiding is staying inside a love that was there first.

Jeremiah 17:7–8

Blessed is the one who trusts in the Lord, like a tree planted by water that does not fear when the heat comes. The Old Testament picture of the abiding life.

Psalm 1:3

A tree planted by streams of water, yielding fruit in its season, its leaf never withering. Rootedness produces fruit that lasts.

Galatians 5:22–23

The fruit of the Spirit. What grows on a branch that stays joined to the vine. Love, joy, peace, and all the rest.

John 5:19

The Son does only what he sees the Father doing. Jesus modeled abiding before he ever asked it of us.
